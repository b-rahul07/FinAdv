Public Provident Fund (PPF)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What it is
PPF is a government-backed savings account for long-term, safe saving. Your money and the interest are guaranteed by the Government of India, which makes it one of the safest long-term options available to individuals. You can open one at a post office or most banks, or online through your bank.

Interest rate
The government sets the rate every quarter. For October to December 2026 it is 7.1% a year, compounded yearly; it has stayed at 7.1% for many quarters. Interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of each month, so deposits made by the 5th earn interest for that month.

How much and for how long
- Minimum ₹500 and maximum ₹1,50,000 in a financial year, in one go or in instalments.
- The account runs for 15 full financial years. After that you can extend it in blocks of 5 years, with or without new deposits.
- One PPF account per person. You can open one for a minor child, but the ₹1.5 lakh limit covers your account and the child's together.

Getting money out
- Partial withdrawal is allowed from the 7th financial year (after 5 full years), up to a limit based on earlier balances, once a year.
- A loan against the balance is possible from the 3rd to the 6th year.
- Closing early is allowed after 5 years only for specific reasons such as serious illness or higher education, with a 1% interest cut.
Because of this, PPF suits money you will not need for a long time, not your emergency fund.

Tax
Interest and the maturity amount are fully tax-free. Deposits can be deducted under section 123 (formerly 80C) only if you choose the old tax regime; under the new regime PPF still gives tax-free, guaranteed returns but no deduction.

When PPF makes sense
- The safe, fixed-income part of a long-term goal such as retirement or a child's education.
- People who want guaranteed returns and are comfortable locking money away.
- Depositing early in the financial year (by 5 April) earns the most interest.

Limits to keep in mind
The rate can change every quarter, the money is locked for long, and over long periods equity funds have usually grown faster (with more ups and downs). Many plans combine PPF for safety with equity funds for growth.
