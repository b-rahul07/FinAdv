ELSS: Equity Linked Savings Schemes (Tax-Saving Mutual Funds)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What it is
An ELSS is a diversified equity mutual fund with a 3-year lock-in. SEBI rules require it to keep at least 80% in shares. It was designed as the equity option for the tax deduction now in section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 80C).

The lock-in
Each investment is locked for 3 years from its own date. With a SIP, every monthly instalment has its own lock-in: the January 2026 instalment can be redeemed from January 2029, the February one from February 2029, and so on. Nobody can withdraw early, even in an emergency, so never put emergency money in ELSS.

Tax
- Deduction: investments count towards the ₹1,50,000 limit only under the old tax regime. Under the new regime there is no deduction, and ELSS works like any other equity fund that happens to have a lock-in.
- Gains: because units are held for 3 years, gains are long-term. Long-term gains on equity funds and shares above ₹1,25,000 a year (across all of them together) are taxed at 12.5%.
- IDCW payouts (formerly dividends) are taxed at your slab rate; most investors prefer the growth option.

Risk and returns
ELSS invests in shares, so its value rises and falls with the stock market and can drop sharply in some years. Returns are not guaranteed. On FinAdvisor's riskometer scale it is a Very High risk product, which is why FinAdvisor suggests it only when your capacity for loss and your questionnaire both allow equity.

ELSS compared with other tax-saving options
- PPF: guaranteed and tax-free, 15-year account, much lower risk, usually lower long-run growth.
- 5-year tax-saver FD: guaranteed, interest taxed at your slab.
- NPS: retirement-only, locked until 60, extra deductions.
- ELSS: shortest lock-in (3 years) and highest potential growth, with the highest risk.

Choosing an ELSS fund
- Pick the Direct plan with the Growth option for the lowest cost.
- Compare expense ratios and long-term consistency, not last year's return.
- One or two ELSS funds are enough; more adds no real diversification.
- After 3 years you are not forced to sell; units can stay invested.
