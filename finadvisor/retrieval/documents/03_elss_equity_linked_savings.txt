Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) — Complete Guide

Overview
Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) is a type of mutual fund that invests primarily in equity and equity-related instruments. ELSS funds are one of the eligible tax-saving instruments under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961. They offer the dual benefit of wealth creation through equity market participation and tax savings.

Key Features
- ELSS funds must invest at least 80% of their total assets in equity and equity-related instruments as per SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) guidelines.
- They are open-ended funds, meaning investors can invest at any time (though each investment has its own lock-in).
- Available in both growth and IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal, formerly called dividend) options.

Lock-in Period
ELSS has the shortest lock-in period among all Section 80C instruments — just 3 years. Each SIP installment is treated as a fresh investment and has its own 3-year lock-in period. For example, if you start a SIP in January 2024, the January 2024 installment is locked in until January 2027, the February 2024 installment until February 2027, and so on.

Tax Benefits
- Investment: Deduction under Section 80C up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year.
- Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): Since the lock-in is 3 years, all ELSS redemptions are long-term. LTCG up to ₹1,25,000 per financial year (across all equity instruments) is tax-exempt. Gains exceeding ₹1,25,000 are taxed at 12.5% (as per the Finance Act, 2024 amendments).
- Dividends: Taxable at the investor's applicable income tax slab rate.

Returns
ELSS returns are market-linked and not guaranteed. Historically, ELSS funds have delivered average annual returns in the range of 12–15% over 10+ year periods, but past performance does not guarantee future results. Returns can be negative in any given year.

Risk Profile
ELSS funds carry equity market risk. They are suitable for investors with:
- A minimum investment horizon of 3 years (lock-in) but ideally 5+ years for meaningful wealth creation
- Moderate to aggressive risk tolerance
- Willingness to accept short-term volatility for potentially higher long-term returns

ELSS vs Other 80C Instruments
Compared to PPF (15-year lock-in, ~7.1% fixed), NSC (5-year lock-in, ~7.7% fixed), and tax-saving FDs (5-year lock-in, 6–7% fixed), ELSS offers:
- Shortest lock-in (3 years)
- Highest return potential (market-linked)
- Highest risk (equity exposure)

SEBI Regulations
- ELSS funds are regulated by SEBI under the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996.
- Fund houses must clearly categorize ELSS as a distinct category.
- There is no upper limit on investment in ELSS, but the 80C tax deduction is capped at ₹1,50,000.

How to Invest
- Through mutual fund AMCs (Asset Management Companies) directly
- Through registered distributors and financial advisors
- Through online platforms and apps registered with AMFI (Association of Mutual Funds in India)
- Minimum investment: Typically ₹500 for SIP, ₹500 for lump sum (varies by fund house)
