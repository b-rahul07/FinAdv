SIP: Systematic Investment Plan
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What a SIP is
A SIP is a standing instruction to invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund at a regular interval, usually monthly. The money is debited from your bank account by auto-debit (NACH or UPI AutoPay) and units are bought at that day's NAV. A SIP is not a product; it is a way of buying mutual fund units.

Why monthly investing helps
- Habit: investing on payday, before spending, makes saving automatic.
- Rupee cost averaging: the same amount buys more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, so you do not need to time the market. Over a long period this smooths out the ups and downs; it does not guarantee a profit.
- Small start: many funds accept SIPs from ₹100 to ₹500 a month.

Setting one up
1. Complete KYC once (PAN, Aadhaar-based e-KYC or in-person).
2. Choose the fund and the Direct plan with the Growth option for the lowest cost.
3. Pick the amount, the date (a few days after salary credit) and the duration; "until cancelled" is common.
4. Approve the auto-debit mandate.
You can buy directly from the fund house's website or app, through MF Central, or through a registered platform.

Changing or stopping
You can pause, change or cancel a SIP at any time without penalty. Missing an instalment does not cause a fine from the fund, though your bank may charge for a bounced debit. Units already bought stay invested. A step-up (top-up) SIP increases the amount automatically every year, for example by 10% when your salary rises.

SIPs and market falls
Stopping a SIP when markets fall is the most common mistake. During a fall each instalment buys more units, and those units benefit most when markets recover. Keep SIPs for goals at least 5 years away going, as long as your emergency fund is in place and you can afford the instalment.

SIP or lump sum?
Money you earn every month is naturally invested by SIP. For a large one-time amount (a bonus or savings above your emergency fund), spreading it over 6 to 12 months, for example through a Systematic Transfer Plan from a liquid fund, reduces the risk of investing everything just before a fall.

Taxes and lock-ins
Each SIP instalment is a separate purchase for tax and lock-in purposes: in ELSS each instalment is locked for 3 years, and the holding period for capital gains is counted per instalment.
