NPS: National Pension System
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What it is
NPS is a low-cost, government-regulated retirement account run under the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). You contribute while working; the money is invested by pension fund managers in equity, corporate bonds and government bonds; at retirement you take part as a lump sum and use the rest for a pension. Any Indian citizen aged 18 to 70 can open one, online through eNPS or through a bank.

Accounts and choices
- Tier I: the main retirement account, locked until 60 except for limited withdrawals. Tax benefits apply here.
- Tier II: an optional, flexible savings account without the tax benefits (except a special version for government employees).
- Investment choice: "Auto" choice reduces equity as you age; "Active" choice lets you set the mix. Since October 2025 the Multiple Scheme Framework lets non-government subscribers hold more than one scheme under the same account, including options with higher equity.

Getting money out at 60 (rules from December 2025)
- Non-government subscribers can take up to 80% of the corpus as a lump sum and must use at least 20% to buy an annuity (a monthly pension). Earlier the split was 60:40.
- Government employees: up to 60% lump sum, at least 40% annuity.
- If the corpus is ₹8 lakh or less, all of it can be withdrawn. Between ₹8 lakh and ₹12 lakh, part can be taken as a lump sum and the rest as an annuity or a systematic withdrawal over several years.
- You can defer the withdrawal and keep the money invested beyond 60.

Leaving before 60
Exiting early usually means at least 80% goes into an annuity and only 20% comes as a lump sum; small balances (₹5 lakh or less) can be withdrawn fully. Partial withdrawals of your own contributions are allowed for specific needs such as children's education, marriage, buying a house or serious illness, a limited number of times.

Tax
- Your own contribution counts within the ₹1.5 lakh deduction (old regime), plus an extra ₹50,000 deduction only for NPS (old regime).
- Your employer's contribution is deductible in both regimes, up to 14% of salary in the new regime.
- Part of the lump sum at retirement has been tax-free; check how the larger 80% lump sum is taxed before you withdraw. Annuity income is taxed at your slab rate.

Who it suits
People who want a disciplined, very low-cost retirement product and do not need the money before 60, especially if the employer contributes. Its main drawbacks are the long lock-in and the compulsory annuity part.
