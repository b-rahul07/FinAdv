Bank Fixed Deposits (FDs)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What an FD is
You lend a lump sum to a bank for a fixed period, from 7 days to 10 years, at an interest rate fixed on the day you open it. The rate does not change for that deposit even if the bank changes its rates later. Senior citizens usually get about 0.25 to 0.5 percentage points more.

How safe is it?
Deposits in banks are insured by the DICGC (a subsidiary of the RBI) up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank, covering principal and interest together across all branches of that bank. If you hold more than ₹5 lakh, spreading it across different banks keeps all of it insured. Small finance banks are covered too, but company FDs and NBFC deposits are not covered by DICGC; their safety depends on the company's credit rating.

Interest and payouts
- Cumulative FDs add interest to the deposit and pay everything at maturity.
- Non-cumulative FDs pay interest monthly or quarterly, useful for regular income.
- Compare banks using the effective annual yield, not just the headline rate.

Tax
- FD interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate, whether it is paid out or added to the deposit.
- Banks deduct TDS of 10% when your interest from that bank in a year crosses the threshold (₹50,000 a year for most people and ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens after Budget 2025). TDS is not the final tax; you still declare the interest in your return.
- If your total income is below the taxable limit, submit Form 15G (or 15H for senior citizens) to avoid TDS.
- The 5-year tax-saver FD qualifies for the section 123 deduction (formerly 80C) under the old regime but cannot be broken early.

Breaking an FD early
Most banks allow premature withdrawal with a penalty, often 0.5% to 1% off the rate. A sweep-in FD linked to your savings account breaks automatically in small amounts when you need money, which suits part of an emergency fund.

When FDs fit
- Money needed within 1 to 3 years with zero tolerance for loss.
- Part of an emergency fund.
- Retirees who want predictable income.
After tax and inflation, FD returns are often close to zero in real terms, so they are not the best home for long-term goals.

Laddering
Splitting a large amount into FDs of different maturities (1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 years) means some money matures every year, reducing the risk of locking everything in at a low rate.
