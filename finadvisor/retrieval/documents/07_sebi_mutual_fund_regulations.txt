How SEBI Regulates Mutual Funds (2026 Rules)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

Who regulates mutual funds
The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) regulates every mutual fund in India. Each fund house (asset management company, AMC) runs schemes under a trust, the money is held by an independent custodian, and the fund house itself cannot use investors' money. AMFI, the industry body, registers mutual fund distributors (ARN holders) and runs investor education. From 1 April 2026 the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 replaced the 1996 regulations.

Costs: what changed in 2026
- The cost you pay is the Total Expense Ratio (TER), deducted daily from the fund's NAV. From April 2026 it is made up of a Base Expense Ratio (the fund house's fees, with caps) plus brokerage, regulatory levies and statutory levies such as GST, which are charged at actual cost on top.
- The caps were lowered. For example, index funds and ETFs can charge at most 0.90% as base expenses (earlier 1.00%), and the cap for smaller equity schemes fell from 2.25% to 2.10%. Larger schemes have lower caps.
- Brokerage that can be charged to the scheme was cut, and the extra allowance some schemes got for charging exit loads was removed. Any exit load collected goes back into the scheme.
- Expense ratios of every scheme are published daily on AMFI's website.

Direct and Regular plans
Every scheme has a Direct plan (bought from the fund house, MF Central or a registered investment adviser, no commission) and a Regular plan (bought through a distributor, who is paid a commission from the expense ratio). Same portfolio, but the Regular plan's expense ratio is higher, typically by 0.5 to 1 percentage point a year.

Scheme categories
SEBI defines categories so that names mean the same thing everywhere: for example large cap (top 100 companies), mid cap, small cap, flexi cap, ELSS, index funds, liquid, overnight, short duration, corporate bond, gilt and balanced advantage. A fund house can generally have only one scheme per category, which makes comparisons fair.

The riskometer
Every scheme shows a riskometer with six levels: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High and Very High. It is reviewed monthly based on what the fund actually holds. FinAdvisor uses this scale to decide which products suit your risk level.

Other investor protections
- Fund houses must disclose portfolios, NAVs (daily) and risk changes.
- Investors receive a Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) every month there is a transaction.
- Complaints go first to the fund house, then to SEBI's SCORES portal.
