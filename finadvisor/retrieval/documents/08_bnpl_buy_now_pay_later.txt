Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) and No-Cost EMIs
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What BNPL is
Buy Now, Pay Later lets you pay for a purchase later or in a few instalments, often at checkout on shopping and food apps. It is a loan: the lender is usually a bank or an NBFC partnered with the app. "No-cost EMI" is a similar idea where the interest is offset by a discount from the seller, so the total you pay should equal the price, but processing fees and GST on interest can still apply.

How it can cost you
- Late fees and penalty interest if you miss the due date, often much higher than a credit card's rate.
- Processing or convenience fees that make a "free" EMI cost extra.
- Several small BNPL plans across apps are easy to lose track of, and together they can strain your monthly budget.

Credit score impact
BNPL accounts are reported to credit bureaus like any loan. Since January 2025 lenders must update bureaus every 15 days, so a missed payment shows up on your credit report quickly and can lower your score for years. Paying on time builds a credit history; missing payments damages it.

Your protections
RBI's digital lending rules require that the lender is a regulated bank or NBFC, that you get a Key Facts Statement with the all-in annual cost (APR) before you agree, that loan money and repayments move directly between you and the lender, and that you can exit within a cooling-off period by repaying the principal and proportionate cost. Recovery agents must follow RBI's conduct rules; harassment can be reported.

When BNPL makes sense, and when not
- Reasonable: a planned purchase you could pay for today anyway, split over a few months, with zero interest and no fee, paid on time.
- Avoid: buying things you can't afford, stacking several plans, or using BNPL while you have unpaid credit card dues or no emergency fund.
FinAdvisor counts any new EMI against the safe limit of 40% of take-home pay for all EMIs together.

Before you click "pay later"
1. Check the lender's name and the Key Facts Statement.
2. Add up all your EMIs including this one.
3. Set a reminder or auto-pay for the due date.
4. Prefer saving up for wants; use BNPL only as a convenience, not as credit you need.
