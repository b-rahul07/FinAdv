Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB) — Complete Guide

Overview
Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are government securities denominated in grams of gold. They are issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the Government of India and offer an alternative to holding physical gold.

Key Features
- Investors pay the issue price in cash, and the bonds are redeemed in cash on maturity.
- Issued in denominations of 1 gram of gold and multiples thereof.
- Minimum investment: 1 gram.
- Maximum limit: 4 kg for individuals, 4 kg for HUFs, and 20 kg for trusts and similar entities per financial year.

Interest Rate
- SGBs bear interest at a fixed rate (currently 2.50% per annum on the amount of initial investment).
- Interest is credited semi-annually to the bank account of the investor.

Tenure and Premature Redemption
- The tenure of the bond is 8 years.
- Premature encashment/redemption is allowed after the 5th year from the date of issue, on coupon payment dates.

Tax Implications
- Interest on the bonds is taxable as per the provision of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (slab rate).
- Capital gains tax arising on redemption of SGB to an individual has been exempted. The exemption on capital gains is one of the biggest advantages of holding SGBs till maturity.
- If transferred before maturity (e.g., sold on exchanges), long-term capital gains (if held for >36 months) are taxed at 12.5% without indexation (as per new budget).

Tradability
- Bonds are tradable on stock exchanges within a fortnight of the issuance on a date as notified by the RBI.

Advantages Over Physical Gold
- No making charges or GST at the time of purchase.
- No risk and cost of storage (theft risk).
- Earns guaranteed interest in addition to gold price appreciation.
- Exempt from capital gains tax upon maturity.
