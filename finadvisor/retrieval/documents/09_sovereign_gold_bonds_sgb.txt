Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

No new issues
The government has not issued any new Sovereign Gold Bond tranche since February 2024, and no issue calendar has been announced for 2026-27. You can no longer buy SGBs from the RBI or banks. Existing bonds can still be bought and sold on the stock exchanges (NSE, BSE) through a demat account, usually at a price close to the gold price, though trading volumes can be thin.

How the bonds work
- Each bond represents one gram of gold and is issued by the RBI on behalf of the Government of India.
- The tenure is 8 years. At maturity you receive the then-current gold price in rupees, not physical gold.
- They pay 2.5% a year interest on the issue price, every six months. This interest is taxable at your slab rate.

Early redemption
Bonds can be redeemed early through the RBI after 5 years from the issue date, only on interest-payment dates in the redemption window that the RBI announces for each tranche. Several older tranches have early-redemption windows during 2026. You apply through the bank, post office or broker where you hold the bond.

Tax
Capital gains on redemption at maturity by an individual have been tax-free. The tax treatment of early redemptions and exchange sales differs and was reported to have changed in 2026, so check the current rules before selling. Gains on sale on the exchange after 12 months are long-term.

If you hold SGBs
Holding to maturity keeps the interest and the tax benefit. Selling on the exchange gives liquidity but may be at a small discount to the gold price.

If you want gold now
Gold ETFs and gold mutual funds (fund of funds) are the simplest regulated ways to add gold today. See FinAdvisor's guide on gold investment options. Most plans keep gold to a small part of long-term money (around 5% to 10%).
