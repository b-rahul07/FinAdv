Emergency Fund: How Much and Where to Keep It
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What an emergency fund is for
An emergency fund is money set aside only for surprises: losing your job, a medical bill beyond insurance, an urgent home or vehicle repair, or supporting family in a crisis. Without one, an emergency forces you to borrow on a credit card or personal loan, or to sell investments at a bad time.

How much
FinAdvisor's plan uses 6 months of your monthly expenses (including EMIs you must keep paying). Consider more, up to 9 to 12 months, if your income is irregular, you are the only earner, you have dependants, or your job is in a volatile industry. Build it before investing for long-term goals; FinAdvisor's plan puts it right after basic insurance and paying off costly debt.

Where to keep it: safe and quick to reach
- Savings account: instant access, about 2.5% to 4% interest, insured up to ₹5 lakh per bank by DICGC.
- Sweep-in FD: a fixed deposit linked to your savings account that breaks automatically when needed; earns FD rates on idle money.
- Liquid mutual funds: invest in very short-term debt (up to 91 days); returns are usually a little above savings accounts; most fund houses allow instant redemption of up to ₹50,000 a day, the rest arrives the next working day.
- Overnight funds: even lower risk than liquid funds, slightly lower returns.
A common split is one month of expenses in the savings account and the rest in a sweep-in FD or a liquid fund.

Where not to keep it
Equity funds, ELSS, PPF, NPS, long FDs with heavy penalties, gold, crypto or a friend's business. These can fall in value or lock your money exactly when you need it.

Tax on liquid funds
Gains on debt funds, including liquid funds, bought from April 2023 are taxed at your income-tax slab rate whatever the holding period, the same as FD interest. A small exit load applies if you redeem a liquid fund within 7 days.

Rebuild after you use it
If you dip into the fund, refilling it becomes the first step of your monthly savings again before new investing.
