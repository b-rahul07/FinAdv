Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme (SCSS)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What it is
SCSS is a government-backed savings scheme for retirees that pays a fixed interest every quarter. It is among the safest sources of regular income for older people and is available at post offices and authorised banks. If your parents are retired, it is often one of the first products to look at for them.

Who can open it
- Anyone aged 60 or above.
- People aged 55 to 60 who retired under voluntary or superannuation retirement, if they invest within a month of receiving retirement benefits.
- Retired defence personnel from age 50, under similar conditions.
An account can be single or joint with a spouse.

Interest rate and payout
For October to December 2026 the rate is 8.2% a year, paid quarterly. The rate is fixed for the whole term on the day you invest, even if later quarters change. Interest not withdrawn does not earn extra interest, so it suits people who need income.

Limits and term
- Minimum ₹1,000, maximum ₹30,00,000 per person across all SCSS accounts.
- Term of 5 years, extendable once by 3 years.
- Early closure is allowed with a penalty: 1.5% of the deposit if closed after 1 year, 1% after 2 years.

Tax
Interest is taxable at the slab rate; TDS applies when a senior citizen's interest from a bank or post office crosses ₹1,00,000 a year (submit Form 15H if your total income is below the taxable limit). The deposit counts for the section 123 deduction (formerly 80C) under the old regime.

How it fits with other options
- SCSS: highest guaranteed rate among small savings schemes, quarterly income, 5-year lock.
- Post Office Monthly Income Scheme: monthly income, lower rate, smaller limits.
- Bank FDs for seniors: flexible terms, rates vary by bank, insured up to ₹5 lakh per bank.
- Many retirees combine SCSS for income with a liquid fund or savings account for emergencies.
