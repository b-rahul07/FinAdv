Education Loans
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What they cover
Education loans pay for tuition and related costs (hostel, books, exam fees, travel and laptops for many courses) for studies in India or abroad. Banks and NBFCs both lend; public sector banks often have lower rates, NBFCs often approve faster and lend more without collateral.

Key terms to compare
- Amount and collateral: smaller loans (often up to ₹7.5 lakh) usually need no collateral and may come with a government credit guarantee; bigger loans need a co-borrower (usually a parent) and often property or a deposit as security.
- Margin: the share of costs you pay yourself, often 5% to 15%.
- Moratorium: repayment usually starts after the course plus 6 to 12 months. Interest during the course is often simple interest; paying it while studying makes the loan much cheaper.
- Rate: usually floating and linked to a benchmark; check the all-in rate and processing fees in the Key Facts Statement.
- Tenure: up to 10 to 15 years after the moratorium.

Tax benefit
Interest paid on an education loan for yourself, your spouse, your children or a student you are the legal guardian of is deductible under section 129 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly 80E), only under the old tax regime. There is no upper limit, and the deduction runs for the first 8 years of repayment or until the interest is paid off, whichever is earlier. Only loans from banks and approved institutions qualify, and only the interest, not the principal.

Government support
Interest subsidies exist for students from lower-income families on loans from banks under government schemes; the Vidya Lakshmi portal lets you apply to several banks at once. Check eligibility before applying.

Repaying sensibly
- Start paying the interest during the course if you can.
- Prepaying a floating-rate education loan carries no penalty under RBI rules.
- Before investing for long-term goals, compare the loan's interest rate (after any tax benefit) with what your investments are expected to earn.
