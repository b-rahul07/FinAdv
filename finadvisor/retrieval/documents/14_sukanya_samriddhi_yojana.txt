Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What it is
SSY is a government-backed savings scheme for a girl child, aimed at her education and marriage. It offers one of the highest guaranteed rates among small savings schemes and fully tax-free returns. Accounts are opened at post offices and authorised banks.

Who can open it
A parent or legal guardian can open one account per girl, for up to two girls (three in the case of twins or triplets), any time before the girl turns 10.

Interest rate
For October to December 2026 the rate is 8.2% a year, compounded yearly. The government reviews the rate every quarter, and the current rate applies to the whole balance.

Deposits
- Minimum ₹250 and maximum ₹1,50,000 in a financial year.
- Deposits are made for 15 years from opening; the account then keeps earning interest until it matures 21 years after opening.
- Missing the yearly minimum makes the account "defaulted"; it can be revived with a small penalty.

Withdrawals
- Up to 50% of the balance at the end of the previous year can be withdrawn after the girl turns 18 (or passes class 10), for higher education.
- The account can be closed after she turns 18 for her marriage.
- Early closure is allowed in limited cases such as the girl's serious illness or the guardian's death.

Tax
Deposits count for the ₹1,50,000 deduction under section 123 (formerly 80C) under the old regime. Interest and the maturity amount are tax-free in both regimes.

How it fits a plan
SSY suits the safe part of a daughter's education goal, especially if she is very young, because the money is locked and guaranteed. For goals 10 or more years away, many parents combine SSY with equity funds for growth, since SSY alone may not keep up with education inflation.
