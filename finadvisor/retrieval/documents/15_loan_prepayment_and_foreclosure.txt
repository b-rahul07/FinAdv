Loan Prepayment, Foreclosure and Moratorium
Last checked: 2026-11-08

The words
- Prepayment (part-payment): paying extra towards the principal before it is due.
- Foreclosure: paying off the whole loan early and closing it.
- Moratorium: a period when you are allowed not to pay EMIs (for example during a course for education loans); interest usually keeps adding up.

Charges: what RBI allows
Banks and NBFCs cannot charge any prepayment or foreclosure fee on floating-rate loans taken by individuals for personal, non-business purposes, such as most home loans. Under the RBI's Pre-payment Charges on Loans Directions, 2025, from 1 January 2026 this also covers floating-rate business loans to individuals and micro and small enterprises, for loans sanctioned or renewed from that date. Fixed-rate loans can still carry a prepayment charge, which must be shown in the sanction letter and the Key Facts Statement.

Shorter tenure or smaller EMI?
When you prepay, the lender either reduces your EMI or reduces the number of remaining EMIs. Cutting the tenure saves much more interest; cutting the EMI frees up monthly cash. Tell the lender which one you want in writing.

When prepaying is a good idea
- The loan is costly: personal loans, credit card loans and BNPL dues almost always cost more than any safe investment earns.
- Your emergency fund and insurance are already in place.
- Your loan rate is higher than what your plan's investments are expected to earn after tax.
For a low-rate home loan, investing for long-term goals may earn more than the interest you save, especially if you claim home-loan tax deductions under the old regime. FinAdvisor compares your loan rate with your plan's expected return.

Before you foreclose
1. Ask for a foreclosure statement with the exact amount and date.
2. Collect all original property documents within the time the lender promises; RBI rules require their return within 30 days of closure, with compensation for delays.
3. Get a no-dues certificate and check that your credit report shows the loan as closed.

Moratoriums
A moratorium pauses EMIs but rarely pauses interest. Taking one makes the loan more expensive overall, so use it only when you truly need the cash.
