Income Tax Regimes: Old vs New (FY 2026-27)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

Two ways to calculate your tax
India has two income-tax regimes for individuals. The new regime is the default: lower slab rates but almost no deductions. The old regime has higher rates but lets you claim deductions such as EPF, PPF, ELSS, insurance premiums, HRA and home-loan interest. From 1 April 2026 both are set out in the Income-tax Act, 2025, which kept the rates of the previous year.

New regime slabs (FY 2026-27)
- Up to ₹4,00,000: nil
- ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000: 5%
- ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 10%
- ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000: 15%
- ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000: 20%
- ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000: 25%
- Above ₹24,00,000: 30%
A rebate of up to ₹60,000 means no tax if your taxable income is up to ₹12,00,000. Salaried people also get a standard deduction of ₹75,000, so a salary of up to about ₹12,75,000 pays no income tax. Special-rate income, such as capital gains, does not get this rebate.

Old regime slabs (below 60 years)
- Up to ₹2,50,000: nil
- ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5%
- ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 20%
- Above ₹10,00,000: 30%
The rebate makes income up to ₹5,00,000 tax-free, and the standard deduction for salaried people is ₹50,000. A 4% health and education cess is added to the tax in both regimes.

Which one is cheaper for you?
It depends on how much you can deduct. The new regime usually wins for people with few deductions, and for anyone earning up to about ₹12.75 lakh as salary. The old regime can win at higher incomes for people with large deductions, for example a big home-loan interest payment, full ₹1.5 lakh investments, HRA and health insurance together. The only reliable way is to calculate both with your own numbers; the Income Tax Department's calculator does this in a few minutes.

Switching
Salaried people can choose the regime every year when filing the return; tell your employer at the start of the year so the right tax is deducted from salary. People with business income have limited chances to switch back and forth.

Common mistakes
- Buying ELSS, insurance or tax-saver FDs for the deduction while on the new regime, where they save nothing.
- Forgetting the employer's NPS contribution, which is deductible in both regimes.
- Comparing regimes without the standard deduction.
