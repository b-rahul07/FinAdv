The Income-tax Act, 2025: Old and New Section Numbers
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What the new Act is
The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026 and replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961. It is a rewrite in simpler language with fewer sections; it did not change tax rates or the main deductions. One visible change is the term "tax year", which replaces the old pair of "previous year" and "assessment year".

Which numbers apply when
- Income earned up to 31 March 2026 (FY 2025-26), including the return you file in 2026: the old 1961 Act numbers.
- Income earned from 1 April 2026 (FY 2026-27) onwards: the new section numbers.

Familiar deductions and their new numbers
- 80C (EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance, home-loan principal and more, ₹1.5 lakh): section 123, with the eligible items listed in Schedule XV.
- 80D (health insurance premiums): section 126.
- 80E (education loan interest): section 129.
- NPS deductions (80CCD): moved to section 124 for the extra ₹50,000 and the employer's contribution; your own contribution also counts within the ₹1.5 lakh limit.
Summaries by tax professionals differ on a few finer points, such as the exact sub-section for each NPS deduction and the new number for home-loan interest, so check the official text or your tax filing utility for those.

What did not change
- The limits: ₹1,50,000 for the main deduction, ₹25,000 or ₹50,000 for health insurance, ₹2,00,000 for home-loan interest on a self-occupied house (old regime).
- The two regimes, and the rule that most deductions apply only under the old regime.
- Capital gains rates on shares and mutual funds.

Why it matters for you
Articles, payslips and forms will start using the new numbers. If you see "section 123", read it as the old 80C; "section 126" is the old 80D. FinAdvisor's guides mention both while people get used to the change.
