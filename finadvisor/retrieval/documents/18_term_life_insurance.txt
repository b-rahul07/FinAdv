Term Life Insurance
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What it is
A term plan pays a fixed sum to your family if you die during the policy term. If you survive the term, nothing is paid back (in a plain term plan). Because it is pure protection, it gives by far the most cover for the premium: a healthy non-smoker in their late 20s can often get ₹1 crore of cover for around the cost of a few restaurant meals a month.

Who needs it
Anyone whose income others depend on: a spouse, children, parents you support, or a co-borrower on a home loan. If no one depends on your income, term cover is optional; FinAdvisor's plan does not ask for it until you have dependants.

How much cover
- FinAdvisor's plan uses about 15 times your annual take-home pay for people with dependants, enough for your family to replace your income for many years.
- Add outstanding loans (home, car) and big goals you want protected, such as a child's education.
- Subtract existing cover and investments your family could use.
- Increase cover after big life events (marriage, a child, a home loan). Many policies let you increase cover at those events without new medical tests.

How long
Until your dependants no longer rely on you, usually until about 60 or your planned retirement. Very long terms (to 85 or 99) cost much more for cover you may not need.

Term plan or investment-cum-insurance?
Endowment, money-back and ULIP policies combine insurance with investing. They give much smaller cover for the same premium, and their returns after charges are usually low. Buying a term plan and investing the difference separately usually leaves your family better protected and your savings more flexible.

Choosing a policy
- Compare insurers' claim settlement ratio and amount settled, published every year by IRDAI.
- Disclose your health, habits (smoking, alcohol) and income honestly; non-disclosure is the most common reason claims are rejected.
- Riders such as accidental death or critical illness are optional extras; compare them with separate policies.
- Consider buying under the Married Women's Property Act (MWPA): the payout then goes only to your wife and children and cannot be claimed by creditors.
- Pay annually if you can; it is usually cheaper than monthly.

Tax
Premiums count towards the section 123 deduction (formerly 80C) under the old regime. The payout to your family is tax-free.
