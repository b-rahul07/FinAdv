Mutual Fund Basics
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What a mutual fund is
A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in shares, bonds or money-market instruments, managed by a professional fund house (AMC) and regulated by SEBI. You own units; the value of each unit is the NAV (net asset value), calculated every business day after markets close. Funds can hold hundreds of companies or bonds, so even a small SIP gets wide diversification.

Main types
- Equity funds: invest mainly in shares (large cap, mid cap, small cap, flexi cap, ELSS, index funds). Highest growth potential, biggest ups and downs; for goals 5 to 7+ years away.
- Debt funds: invest in bonds and money-market instruments (liquid, overnight, short duration, gilt, corporate bond). Steadier, for shorter goals and emergency money.
- Hybrid funds: mix shares and bonds (balanced advantage, aggressive hybrid, equity savings).
- Index funds and ETFs: copy an index such as the Nifty 50 at very low cost.
- Solution-oriented and fund-of-funds: retirement or children's funds, gold funds, international funds.

Direct or Regular plan?
Every scheme comes in a Direct plan and a Regular plan with the same portfolio. The Regular plan pays a commission to the distributor out of its expense ratio, so its returns are lower every year, typically by 0.5 to 1 percentage point. Over 20 years that difference can reduce your final amount by a tenth or more. Direct plans are bought on the fund house's own website or app, on MF Central, or through a SEBI-registered investment adviser.

Growth or IDCW?
- Growth: profits stay invested and compound. Best for most long-term investors.
- IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal, formerly "dividend"): the fund pays out part of your own money from time to time, taxed at your slab rate. It is not extra income.

Costs to know
- Expense ratio (TER): a yearly fee taken daily from the NAV; lower is better, especially for index funds. AMFI publishes every scheme's expense ratio.
- Exit load: a fee if you sell within a set period (often 1% within a year for equity funds; a small charge within 7 days for liquid funds).
- No entry load is charged on any mutual fund in India.

How to read a fund
Check the category, the riskometer, the expense ratio, the fund's age and long-term consistency compared with its benchmark. Past returns, especially last year's, don't predict future returns. One or two funds per category are enough.

Getting started
Complete KYC once, choose the fund and plan, then invest by SIP or lump sum. Your holdings across all fund houses appear in a monthly Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) from CAMS and KFintech or the depositories.
