Post Office Savings Schemes (NSC, KVP, MIS, RD, Time Deposits)
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What they are
India Post runs several small savings schemes backed by the Government of India, so your money and interest are fully safe. Rates are set by the government every quarter; for the fixed-term schemes below, the rate on the day you invest stays for the whole term.

Rates for October to December 2026
- National Savings Certificate (NSC): 7.7%, 5 years, interest compounded yearly and paid at maturity.
- Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP): 7.5%; your money doubles in about 115 months.
- Monthly Income Scheme (MIS): 7.4%, paid monthly, 5 years.
- Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme: 8.2% (see the SCSS guide).
- PPF 7.1% and Sukanya Samriddhi 8.2% (see their guides).
- Post office savings account: 4%.
Recurring deposits (5 years) and time deposits (1, 2, 3 and 5 years) are also available; check the current rates at the post office or India Post's website.

Limits and access
- MIS: up to ₹9 lakh in a single account and ₹15 lakh in a joint account.
- NSC and KVP: no upper limit; KVP needs PAN and other documents for larger amounts.
- Early closure is possible after a minimum period, usually with an interest cut.
Many schemes can now be opened and managed through India Post's internet banking or the IPPB app.

Tax
- Interest on NSC, KVP, MIS, RD and time deposits is taxable at your slab rate.
- NSC and the 5-year time deposit count for the section 123 deduction (formerly 80C) under the old regime.
- PPF and Sukanya Samriddhi interest is tax-free.

When they fit
For safe, fixed-income money with a known end date, especially for people who prefer the post office, or for parents and retirees who want guaranteed income. For long-term growth, equity funds have usually done better, with more ups and downs. Compare post-tax returns with bank FDs and debt funds before choosing.
