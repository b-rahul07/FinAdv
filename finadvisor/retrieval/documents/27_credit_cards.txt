Using Credit Cards Safely
Last checked: 2026-11-08

How a credit card bill works
Each billing cycle (about a month) ends with a statement showing the total amount due, the minimum amount due and the payment due date, usually 15 to 20 days later. Purchases get an interest-free period of roughly 20 to 50 days, but only if you pay the total amount due in full by the due date.

The minimum-due trap
Paying only the minimum avoids a late fee, but interest is charged on the whole unpaid balance and on new purchases from the day you make them, at around 3% to 3.75% a month, which is roughly 36% to 45% a year. A ₹50,000 balance can take years to clear on minimum payments. Card debt is the costliest debt most people ever carry; clearing it comes before any investing in FinAdvisor's plan.

Costs to know
- Interest (finance charges) on unpaid balances and on cash withdrawals, from day one.
- Late payment fees and GST on fees and interest.
- Cash withdrawal fees; using a card at an ATM is almost always a bad idea.
- Annual fees, often waived if you spend a set amount.
- Foreign currency mark-up on international spends.
RBI rules require a one-page Key Fact Statement with the interest rate and charges when you apply.

Your rights under RBI rules
- The card issuer must close your card within 7 working days of your request if no dues are pending; if it doesn't, it pays you a penalty for each day of delay.
- Cards cannot be issued or upgraded without your explicit consent.
- Your consent is needed before your credit limit is raised.
- Report a lost card or fraud immediately; your liability is limited if you report quickly.

Using a card well
1. Set up auto-pay for the full amount, not the minimum.
2. Spend only what you would spend from your bank account anyway.
3. Keep usage under about 30% of the limit for a healthy credit score.
4. Rewards rarely outweigh interest; they only make sense if you never carry a balance.

If you already have card debt
Stop using the card for new spends, pay the highest-interest balance first, and consider converting it into a lower-rate EMI or paying it off with a cheaper personal loan only if you won't run the card up again. FinAdvisor's plan schedules card dues before anything else.
