Paying Off Debt: Which Loan First?
Last checked: 2026-11-08

Know what you owe
List every debt with its outstanding amount, interest rate and EMI: credit cards, BNPL, personal loans, car or two-wheeler loans, education and home loans, gold loans and money borrowed from friends. Add up all EMIs and compare them with your take-home pay; FinAdvisor treats 40% as the safe limit for all EMIs together.

Costly debt first
Interest you avoid by repaying a loan is a guaranteed return. Paying off a credit card at 40% a year is like earning 40% risk-free, which no investment can match. As a rough guide:
- Credit card dues, BNPL penalties and payday or app loans: clear first, before investing.
- Personal loans (often 11% to 24%): usually worth prepaying before long-term investing beyond your emergency fund.
- Car loans: prepay if the rate is above what your plan's investments are expected to earn.
- Home and education loans: often the cheapest debt, with tax benefits under the old regime; investing alongside them is usually fine.

Two popular methods
- Avalanche: pay the minimum on everything and put every extra rupee into the highest-interest debt. Saves the most money.
- Snowball: put extra money into the smallest debt first for quick wins, then roll that EMI into the next. Costs a little more but keeps some people motivated.
Either works if you stick to it; the avalanche is cheaper.

Ways to cut the cost
- Balance transfer: move card or personal-loan debt to a lender with a lower rate, but check fees and avoid running up the old card again.
- Convert card dues to an EMI at a lower rate.
- Use a bonus, maturing FD or savings above your emergency fund to prepay costly debt.
- Never take a new loan to invest, or to pay another loan's EMIs month after month.

If you are struggling
Talk to your lender before missing payments; restructuring, a lower EMI or a longer tenure may be possible. Avoid "debt settlement" offers that leave a "settled" mark on your credit report. Recovery agents must follow RBI's code of conduct; report harassment to the lender and, if unresolved, to the RBI Ombudsman.

After the debt is gone
Redirect the old EMI amount into your emergency fund and then your goals, so your lifestyle doesn't quietly absorb it.
