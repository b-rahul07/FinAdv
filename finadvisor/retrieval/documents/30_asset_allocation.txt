Asset Allocation and Rebalancing
Last checked: 2026-11-08

What asset allocation means
Asset allocation is how you split your money between asset types: equity (shares and equity funds), debt (FDs, PPF, EPF, debt funds), gold and cash. It decides most of your portfolio's long-term return and how much it can fall in a bad year, much more than which individual fund you pick.

What decides your mix
- Time until you need the money: the main factor. Money needed within 3 years belongs in safe debt and cash; 3 to 7 years can mix in some equity; 7 years or more can hold mostly equity if your risk level allows.
- Capacity for loss: your emergency fund, insurance, debts and dependants decide how big a fall you can afford.
- Willingness to take risk: how you would actually feel and act in a crash.
FinAdvisor sets your risk level as the lower of your capacity and your willingness, and builds a separate mix for each goal based on its horizon.

Rules of thumb, used with care
Formulas like "100 minus your age in equity" are rough starting points, not advice for your situation. A 25-year-old saving for a home in 3 years needs a cautious mix for that goal, even if their retirement money can be mostly equity.

Diversify inside each asset
- Equity: a broad index fund or a flexi-cap fund spreads money over many companies; adding many similar funds doesn't add diversification.
- Debt: mix EPF, PPF and short-duration funds or FDs; avoid lending all your money to one company.
- Gold: a small share, usually 5% to 10%.
Individual shares concentrate risk; keep them a small part if you hold any.

Rebalancing
Over time, the asset that rises most takes up a bigger share than planned. Rebalancing brings the mix back to target, which quietly makes you sell some of what went up and buy what fell.
- When: once a year, or when an asset drifts more than about 5 percentage points from target.
- How: first redirect new SIPs towards the asset that is below target; sell only if needed, since selling can create tax.
- As a goal gets close, shift gradually from equity to debt (a "glide path") so a late crash can't derail it.
