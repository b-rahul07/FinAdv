Investing When Markets Fall
Last checked: 2026-11-08

Falls are normal
Stock markets fall regularly. A 10% drop happens often, sometimes several times a year; falls of 20% to 40% have happened every few years in India, for example in 2008, 2020 and several shorter episodes since. Markets have recovered from every past fall over time, but recoveries have taken anywhere from months to a few years, and nobody can promise the future will be the same.

What usually works
- Keep SIPs going for goals at least 5 years away. During a fall each instalment buys more units, and those units gain the most in a recovery.
- Don't sell in panic. Selling turns a temporary paper loss into a real one and usually means missing the rebound, which often comes suddenly.
- Check your plan, not the headlines: money you need within 3 years should not be in equity anyway, so a fall shouldn't touch your near-term goals.
- Rebalance: if equity has fallen well below your target share, a planned top-up from other assets is a disciplined way to buy low.

What doesn't work
- Timing the market: waiting for the bottom or selling before the next drop. Even professionals rarely manage it consistently, and the best days often come right after the worst ones.
- Chasing whatever fell most, or switching to a "safer" fund after the damage is done.
- Investing borrowed money or your emergency fund to "buy the dip".

When stopping or reducing a SIP makes sense
- You've lost income or your emergency fund is short: protect yourself first and restart when stable.
- The goal is now less than 2 to 3 years away: move that goal's money gradually to safer assets, a decision about the goal, not the market.
- You can't afford the instalment because of a costly debt; clear it first.

Keeping calm
Look at your portfolio less often during a fall, remind yourself why each investment exists and when you'll need it, and remember that your willingness questionnaire is meant to match how you'd feel in exactly this situation. If a fall makes you want to sell everything, your mix may be riskier than you're comfortable with; adjust it calmly after markets settle, not in a panic.
