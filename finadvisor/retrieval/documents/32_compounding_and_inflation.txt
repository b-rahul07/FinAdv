Compounding and Inflation
Last checked: 2026-11-08

Compounding
Compounding means earning returns on your returns. If you invest ₹1,00,000 at 10% a year, you have ₹1,10,000 after a year; the next year's 10% is earned on ₹1,10,000, and so on. After 10 years it is about ₹2,59,000, after 20 years about ₹6,73,000 and after 30 years about ₹17,45,000. Most of the growth comes in the later years, which is why starting early matters more than investing large amounts later.

The rule of 72
Divide 72 by the yearly return to estimate how many years money takes to double: about 10 years at 7%, about 7 years at 10%, about 6 years at 12%. It works the other way too: at 6% inflation, prices double in about 12 years.

Starting early
Someone who invests ₹5,000 a month from age 25 to 35 and then stops can end up with more at 60 than someone who invests ₹5,000 a month from 35 to 60, at the same return, because the early money compounds for longer. Small, regular amounts started early beat large amounts started late.

Inflation
Inflation is the rise in prices over time. India's central bank targets 4% consumer inflation with a band of 2% to 6%; FinAdvisor uses 6% for long-term planning to be cautious. At 6%, something that costs ₹1,00,000 today will cost about ₹1,79,000 in 10 years and ₹3,21,000 in 20 years. Education and healthcare costs have often risen faster than general inflation.

Real returns
What matters is your return after inflation (and after tax):
- A savings account at 3% loses value in real terms when inflation is 6%.
- An FD at 7%, taxed at 30%, earns about 4.9% after tax, which is below 6% inflation.
- Long-term equity investing has historically beaten inflation by a wider margin, with much bigger ups and downs along the way.
This is why goals many years away usually need some growth assets, and why FinAdvisor shows goal targets in today's money and in future, inflated money.

Costs compound too
A 1% higher yearly fee, such as a Regular plan instead of a Direct plan, can reduce a 25-year investment's final value by around a fifth. Low costs are one of the few things you control.
