Money Basics for Young Earners: Your First Salary
Last checked: 2026-11-08

The order that protects you
Personal finance works best in a fixed order, because each step protects the next:
1. Know your numbers: take-home pay, fixed expenses, EMIs and what's left.
2. Health insurance for yourself (and family who depend on you), even if your employer covers you.
3. Clear costly debt: credit card dues, BNPL and personal loans.
4. Emergency fund: about 6 months of expenses in a savings account, sweep-in FD or liquid fund.
5. Term insurance once someone depends on your income.
6. Invest for goals: each goal gets its own amount and mix based on when you need the money.
FinAdvisor's plan follows this order and tells you how much goes where each month.

Budgeting without stress
A simple guide is 50/30/20: about half of take-home pay for needs (rent, food, EMIs, bills), up to a third for wants, and at least a fifth for saving and investing. Adjust to your city and situation; the key is to save first, on payday, by automatic transfer or SIP, and spend what's left.

Read your payslip
- EPF: 12% of basic pay is saved for you, and your employer adds 12%; check it reaches your EPF passbook.
- Tax: tell your employer your tax regime at the start of the year; the new regime is the default. Submit investment and rent proofs in time if you choose the old regime.
- Professional tax and other deductions are listed too.

Habits that matter more than products
- Raise your SIP when your salary rises, before your spending grows into the raise.
- Avoid lifestyle EMIs (phones, gadgets, holidays on credit).
- Keep one or two good funds; you don't need many.
- Use a credit card only if you pay the full bill every month.
- Learn before you invest in anything complex; avoid F&O, crypto and tips from social media.

Starting small is fine
₹1,000 a month started at 23 can grow into more than larger amounts started at 35, because of compounding. Start with what you can, and step it up every year.
