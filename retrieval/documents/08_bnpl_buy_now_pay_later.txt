Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) in India — Consumer Guide and Risks

Overview
Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) is a short-term financing option that allows consumers to make purchases and pay for them later, typically in interest-free installments or as a deferred lump sum. In India, BNPL services are offered by fintech companies, banks, and NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies) and have grown rapidly since 2020.

How BNPL Works
1. Consumer selects BNPL at checkout (online or offline).
2. The BNPL provider pays the merchant on behalf of the consumer.
3. The consumer repays the BNPL provider in installments (usually 3–6 monthly installments) or as a lump sum within 15–45 days.
4. If paid on time, there is typically no interest or fee. Late payments incur penalties and interest.

Major BNPL Providers in India
- Simpl, LazyPay (PayU), ZestMoney (ceased operations), Slice (now merged with North East Small Finance Bank), Amazon Pay Later, Flipkart Pay Later, HDFC Bank FlexiPay, Paytm Postpaid, ICICI PayLater.

RBI Regulations (June 2022 Onwards)
- The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issued guidelines in June 2022 restricting non-bank PPI (Prepaid Payment Instrument) issuers from offering credit lines. This significantly impacted several BNPL players.
- BNPL providers must partner with banks or NBFCs to offer credit. They cannot directly lend without being a registered NBFC or bank.
- Digital lending guidelines (September 2022) require:
  - Full disclosure of Annual Percentage Rate (APR) to the borrower
  - Disbursement and repayment directly between borrower's and regulated entity's bank accounts
  - Cooling-off period: Borrowers must be given a look-up/cooling-off period during which they can exit the loan without penalty. This period is typically 1–3 days as determined by the lender.

Interest and Fees
- Most BNPL products advertise "0% interest" for on-time payments.
- Late payment penalties: ₹100 to ₹500 per missed payment, plus interest at 1.5% to 3% per month (18% to 36% APR) on the outstanding amount.
- Processing fees: Some providers charge ₹50 to ₹200 per transaction or a flat monthly fee.
- Longer-tenor BNPL (6–12 months) may carry interest rates of 12% to 24% APR.

Credit Score Impact
- BNPL transactions are increasingly being reported to credit bureaus (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark).
- Missed payments or defaults on BNPL can negatively impact the consumer's credit score.
- Multiple BNPL accounts and high utilization can signal over-leveraging to credit scoring models.

Risks for Consumers
1. Over-spending: The ease of BNPL encourages impulse purchases and spending beyond means.
2. Debt spiral: Multiple BNPL commitments across different platforms can accumulate quickly.
3. Hidden costs: Late fees and interest can make a "free" BNPL purchase expensive.
4. Credit score damage: Defaults are reported to credit bureaus.
5. Lack of budgeting discipline: BNPL can mask the true cost of purchases.

Consumer Protection
- RBI mandates that all BNPL providers (through partner banks/NBFCs) must:
  - Provide a Key Fact Statement (KFS) before loan disbursement
  - Disclose all charges upfront including APR
  - Offer a grievance redressal mechanism
  - Not charge prepayment penalties for loans with tenure ≤ 12 months
- Consumers can file complaints with the RBI Ombudsman if the BNPL provider (regulated entity) fails to resolve grievances.

BNPL and Financial Planning
- Financial advisors recommend limiting BNPL usage to essential purchases only.
- Total BNPL + EMI commitments should not exceed 30–40% of monthly income.
- Building an emergency fund (3–6 months of expenses) should take priority over using BNPL for discretionary spending.
