Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) — Complete Guide

Overview
The Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) is a government-backed savings scheme launched as part of the 'Beti Bachao, Beti Padhao' campaign. It is aimed at encouraging parents to build a fund for the future education and marriage expenses of their female child.

Eligibility and Account Opening
- The account can be opened by the biological parents or legal guardian of a girl child.
- The girl child must be below the age of 10 years at the time of account opening.
- Only one account can be opened per girl child.
- A maximum of two accounts can be opened in a family (for two daughters). In case of twins or triplets at the second birth, more than two accounts may be allowed.

Deposit Rules
- Minimum deposit: ₹250 per financial year.
- Maximum deposit: ₹1,50,000 per financial year.
- Deposits must be made for a period of 15 years from the date of opening the account. After 15 years, no further deposits are allowed, but the account continues to earn interest until maturity.

Maturity Period
- The account matures 21 years from the date of account opening.
- Alternatively, the account can be closed at the time of the girl child's marriage, provided she has attained the age of 18 years. The closure can happen 1 month before or 3 months after the marriage.

Interest Rate
- The interest rate for SSY is reviewed and announced quarterly by the Government of India.
- It typically offers a higher interest rate compared to PPF (often around 8.0% to 8.2%).
- Interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the 5th and the end of the month and is compounded annually.

Partial Withdrawal
- Premature or partial withdrawal is allowed once the girl child attains the age of 18 years or completes her 10th standard, whichever is earlier.
- Withdrawal is permitted only for the purpose of the girl child's higher education.
- Maximum withdrawal limit: 50% of the balance at the end of the preceding financial year.

Tax Benefits
SSY falls under the Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) tax category:
1. Contributions qualify for deduction under Section 80C (up to ₹1,50,000).
2. The interest earned is completely tax-exempt.
3. The maturity amount is entirely tax-free.

Premature Closure
- Premature closure is permitted in extreme compassionate grounds such as the death of the account holder or life-threatening diseases of the account holder, subject to documentation.
